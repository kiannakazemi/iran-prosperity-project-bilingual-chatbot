### **Importance**

Transparent, centralized coordination cuts duplication and deters diversion of scarce supplies. The absence of such coordination created recurrent problems documented in the Syrian conflict, where treatment gaps and medicine shortages accounted for tens of thousands of indirect deaths [2]. Analyses of Libya's civil war likewise indicate that early establishment of field command posts reduced case‑fatality ratios despite higher injury volumes [3].
